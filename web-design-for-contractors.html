Your Website Is Costing You Calls

Most contractor sites I see fail in the same four ways. If any of these sound familiar, that's the leak:

Slow on job-site phones

Your customer is standing in a driveway on weak signal. If your site takes six seconds to load, they're calling the next name on Google.

No after-hours quote path

People shop for contractors at 9pm. No quote form, no calculator, no chat — just a phone number nobody answers until morning. That's a lost job.

Missed calls, no backup

You're on a roof or under a sink — you can't pick up. Without a site that captures the lead anyway, that caller doesn't leave a voicemail. They move on.

Looks like everyone else

Template sites with stock photos of handshakes. Nothing about your crew, your work, or why a homeowner should trust you over the next listing.

Contractor Website FAQ

Straight answers, no sales pitch.

What does a contractor website cost?

Websites start at $900 flat, with a $450 deposit to start and the balance due before launch. Most full custom builds land between $2,200 and $4,000 depending on scope. You get the full price up front after a free scope call — no hourly meter running.

How long does it take to build?

Most contractor websites launch within 2-4 weeks. Branding or AI chat widget add-ons typically add 3-7 days. Because it's just me building it, you're never waiting in a queue behind other clients' projects.

Will my site work on customers' phones?

Yes — every site I build is mobile-first and designed to load fast on job-site phones with weak signal. Your customers are usually standing in a driveway when they look you up, so the phone experience comes first, not last.

How do I get leads after hours?

Two ways I build in: an instant quote calculator so visitors can get a ballpark number without calling, and an AI chat widget trained on your hours, services and pricing that answers common questions 24/7. Both feed into click-to-call and a quote request flow.

I already have a website. Can you fix it instead of rebuilding?

Sometimes, yes. On a free consultation I'll look at what you have and tell you plainly whether a tune-up makes sense or whether you'd be paying to polish something that should be rebuilt. Either way you get an honest answer, not a sales pitch.

Where do you work?

I'm based in Missoula and meet contractors in person across Missoula County, the Bitterroot Valley and the Flathead Valley. The work is remote-friendly by nature, so contractors anywhere in Montana — or anywhere in the US — get the same process and flat pricing.
